Principles of the World Historical Gazetteer

1. What WHG is

WHG is all of the following at once:

  • a reference work
  • a piece of digital infrastructure
  • a publishing platform
  • an authoring platform

Aspiration: to be the go-to source for historical place information. Think "Google Maps for the past."

2. The core data model

2.1 Places are entities, not names

  • Named spatial entities are entities, not just names.
  • The unit of entity is the named spatial entity.
  • A place is defined as a cluster of attestations: its names, location, and feature type.

2.2 Attestations

  • Entities and attestations are linked to one another.
  • Places are never just one thing, so attestations must be linked in order to make sense of them.
  • Individual attestations have authorship.

2.3 Hard links

  • It must be possible to author and hard link attestations to reflect coexistence and/or succession.
  • Links should record why they are hard linked, ideally through an ontology of change types.
  • Places can be hard linked into administrative hierarchies, because places relate to one another in both networks and hierarchies.

2.4 Context

  • Places exist in context with one another and with other characteristics, such as ecological setting.

2.5 Not map-first

  • We are not map-first, and that is the point.
  • It lets us index entities that are difficult to situate in a precise location.

3. Scholarship, authorship, and curation

  • WHG has a scholarly and domain-expert layer. Authored and well-cited submissions are important.
  • Expertise can be deep on a single place.
  • Dataset contributions and individual attestations both carry authorship.
  • Datasets should all have really good metadata.
  • There must be links back to contributor pages, with URIs that point to specific entities.
  • We need to recruit and curate what goes into our index rather than leave it to chance. That means active editing and curation.

4. Our value proposition

  • The value we add is in specialist, artisanal datasets, not in building links between Wikidata and GeoNames.
  • We cannot just be rechewing GeoNames and Wikidata. The project's purpose is not "here's something you could find on Wikidata."
  • We are not a disambiguation engine but a reambiguation engine.
  • This remains a decolonial project.

5. Openness and the API

  • Everything is open data and open source.
  • Openness does not prevent us from licensing large-scale calls to our API.
  • The API is highly valuable and should be marketed as an important way for people and institutions to engage with our index, not only as a way to drive people to the website.

6. Growth

More is better, across the board:

  • more entities
  • more attestations
  • more contributions
  • more users
  • more links and API calls

7. Institutional context

  • For the foreseeable future, WHG faces toward Pitt as funder and host, and outward toward the world.

9. Summary

WHG is a reference work, infrastructure, publishing platform, and authoring platform for historical place information. It treats places as entities built from linked, authored attestations rather than as dots on a map, which lets it hold ambiguity and index places that resist precise location. Its value comes from curated, well-documented, specialist datasets and from an open API, not from re-linking Wikidata and GeoNames. It is open, scholarly, decolonial in orientation, and aims to be the go-to source for historical place information.
