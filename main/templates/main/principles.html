Principles of the World Historical Gazetteer

The World Historical Gazetteer (WHG) is, for historical place information:

  • a reference work
  • a piece of digital infrastructure
  • a publishing platform
  • an authoring platform

It treats places as entities known through linked, authored attestations rather than as dots on a map, which lets it hold ambiguity and index places that resist precise location. Its value comes from curated, well-documented specialist gazetteers and from an open, free-to-use API, not from re-linking Wikidata and GeoNames. These are the principles that guide it.

Aspiration: to be the first place people look for information about historical places.

1. The core data model

1.1 Places are entities, not names

A place is a named spatial entity, known through the attestations made about it: its names, its location and its feature type, each with a source and an author.

1.2 Attestations

An attestation is a single, sourced statement about a place: a name, a location, a type, a date. (PLATO, the data model WHG builds on, defines the term.)

  • Entities and attestations are linked to one another.
  • Places are never just one thing, so attestations must be linked in order to make sense of them.
  • Individual attestations have authorship.

1.3 Authored relations

WHG aims to let people author relations between attestations and places, such as succession, coexistence and part-of:

  • a relation should record why it was made, ideally through an ontology of types of change, which WHG aims to develop;
  • places can be placed in administrative hierarchies, because places relate to one another in both networks and hierarchies.

1.4 Context

Places exist in context with one another and with other characteristics. WHG aims to situate places in context, such as their ecological setting.

1.5 Not map-first

WHG is not map-first. That lets it index entities that are difficult to situate in a precise location.

2. Scholarship, authorship and curation

  • WHG has a scholarly and domain-expert layer. Authored and well-cited submissions are important, and expertise can be deep on a single place.
  • Gazetteer contributions and individual attestations both carry authorship.
  • Every gazetteer should have rich, well-structured metadata.
  • WHG aims to link back to contributor pages, with URIs that point to specific entities.
  • WHG recruits and curates what goes into its index rather than leaving it to chance. That means active editing and curation.

3. Our value proposition

  • The value WHG adds is in specialist, carefully made gazetteers. Linking records to GeoNames and Wikidata is necessary plumbing, but it is not our contribution.
  • WHG is more than a re-presentation of GeoNames and Wikidata: its purpose is the places and the knowledge about them that those sources do not hold.
  • WHG is not only a disambiguation engine but also a way of keeping the ambiguity of historical places visible: competing names, locations and extents are kept side by side instead of being collapsed into one answer.
  • WHG aims to be a decolonial project. For Indigenous place knowledge the default is to withhold rather than publish, until the communities concerned have decided how it may be shared, following the CARE principles for Indigenous data governance.

4. Openness and the API

  • WHG's code is open source (BSD‑3‑Clause) and WHG's own contributions are openly licensed. Every source keeps its own licence, which WHG shows with the data and passes on; see Data and software licences.
  • The API is free to use within published limits. To sustain the service, WHG may offer higher-capacity access on a cost-recovery basis; that would charge for capacity, not license anyone else's data.
  • The API is a first-class way to use WHG, for people and institutions alike, not only a way to bring people to the website.
  • A read-only MCP server lets AI assistants query WHG directly.

5. Growth

WHG aims to grow, without lowering the bar for quality or documentation:

  • more entities
  • more attestations
  • more contributions
  • more users
  • more links and API calls

The detailed requirements that follow from these principles are tracked openly in the V4 requirements checklist.

6. Institutional context

WHG's home is the University of Pittsburgh, its principal institutional supporter (see Funding and awards), and it faces outward to the world.
