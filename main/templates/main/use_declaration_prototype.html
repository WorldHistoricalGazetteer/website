Declared Use Check

WHG holds 51 million place records drawn from 26 authority gazetteers under nine different licences, plus 2.8 million contributed by its own users. Before data leaves the platform we would ask what it is for, and release only those sources whose terms allow it. This is a working sketch of that exchange — and of where it breaks down.

01

Who is declaring?

A declaration made by an anonymous account transfers responsibility to nobody. We need a name, and to know who stands behind it.

Independent researchers are not second-class here. Capacity decides who the declaration binds, not what you receive: an unaffiliated genealogist or local historian is served exactly the same sources as a university. All we lose is the ability to look to an institution if the declaration proves untrue, and a named person answering for themselves is enough.

02

Will the use be commercial?

“Non-commercial” is notoriously undefined, so we ask about the act rather than the category.

If in doubt, treat it as commercial. A grant-funded study whose outputs are published open access is not commercial. Consultancy delivered for a fee, a paid subscription product, or a dataset sold on, all are — even where the buyer is a university.

03

Will you pass the data on?

Attribution and share-alike obligations only work if they travel. This decides whether you take on that duty.

Share-alike engages.

04

Will you alter or combine the records?

Reformatting, merging with your own data, or deriving new records from ours all count as adaptation.

One source forbids adaptations. The Digital Gazetteer of the Song Dynasty is published under CC BY-ND 4.0, so it is withheld from any use that adapts it.

    Why the button says what it says

    The label is not decoration. What the user is asked to do at the moment of clicking is what the record afterwards evidences, so three plainer alternatives were rejected.

    • “I agree to these terms” — frames the click as accepting conditions we are imposing. For material we hold under CC BY 4.0 that is the one thing we must not do: clause 2(a)(5)(B) forbids a redistributor from adding downstream restrictions.
    • “Accept and download” — makes the download the object of the sentence. The declaration is the object; the download is its consequence.
    • “Submit” — names no act at all, and a record of someone having pressed Submit evidences nothing about what they said.

    “Make this declaration” names the act, and names it as a statement of fact rather than a promise. That is what makes an untrue one a misrepresentation, which is the whole basis on which the record is supposed to protect us. The label is the same on both routes, because the act is the same one; only its consequence differs.

    About this sketch. The 26 sources, their SPDX licences and their record counts are the real ones, read from the WHG gazetteer registry on 4 September 2026. The branching, the wording and the stored record are proposals, not policy. One deliberate constraint shapes the drafting: for material we hold under CC BY 4.0 we must not make access conditional on promises going beyond that licence, since clause 2(a)(5)(B) forbids a redistributor from imposing downstream restrictions. So this operates as a declaration of fact, used by us to decide what to release — never as a contractual restriction on what a recipient may afterwards do with material whose licence already permits it.
