✨ Reconciliation Review Screen Guide

This screen is complex. The information displayed and the available options are explained below.

📝 Screen Elements and Functions
  1. The user-designated label of the current dataset.
  2. Save / Defer: Click 'Save' after you have made your match decisions (choosing either 'closeMatch' or 'no match'). Click 'Defer' if you want to skip a decision on the current record; it will be moved to a separate 'deferred' queue so you can revisit it later.
  3. Current Matching Pass: This indicates the stage of the reconciliation process. PASS 0 is included if any automatic matches were made based on shared links, and these can be accepted in bulk from the Linking screen. Otherwise, you will be stepped through potential matches from PASS 1, then PASS 2, and in the case of Getty TGN, potentially PASS 3.
  4. Undo Save: Allows you to **undo the last 'Save'** action; useful if you click 'Save' and then immediately reconsider your decision.
  5. Your Dataset Record: The record from your dataset that was sent for matching. If it has associated location data (geometry), it will appear as **green markers** on the map.
  6. Close Match vs. No Match: The options for linking your record to a potential match. This concept is detailed in the section below.
  7. Geometry Indicator: The **globe icon** indicates that the potential match includes location data (geometry). **Hovering your mouse over the link** for the potential match will highlight its location on the map.
🤝 Understanding 'closeMatch'

The meaning of closeMatch derives from the Simple Knowledge Organization System (SKOS) vocabulary, a widely used data model.

Practical Application in WHG

Practically speaking, for WHG, asserting a 'closeMatch' serves as a linking mechanism or "glue." Specifically, records that share one or more common links asserted as closeMatch are **joined together** in our "union index" and returned together in response to queries. For example, records for Abyssinia and Ethiopia share two closeMatch links, to a DBPedia record and a TGN record. Therefore, they appear together when searching for either Abyssinia or Ethiopia. We have determined that the distinction between SKOS:exactMatch and closeMatch is not clear enough to offer exactMatch as a separate choice.

SKOS Specification Definitions
  • closeMatch: "...(the) two concepts are sufficiently similar that they can be used interchangeably in some information retrieval applications"
  • exactMatch: "...a high degree of confidence that two concepts can be used interchangeably across a wide range of information retrieval applications."

Furthermore, closeMatch is a **super-property** of exactMatch; that is, every exactMatch is also a closeMatch. Remember, the purpose of the assertion is to ensure records that should intuitively appear together, do.
