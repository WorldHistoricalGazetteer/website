GRACE

Palak's editorial tracker: the design, reviewed and settled

Prepared by: Stephen Gadd  ·  Date: 27 August 2026  ·  For: Ruth Mostern, Palak Vashist, Alexandra Straub  ·  Purpose: response to "Tracking people, projects, and datasets — a working design for our editorial workflow"

Palak's draft describes a coherent system. The entity graph in the prose and the diagram agree with each other, the cardinalities are stated and correct, and the "how is it controlled / what values are allowed" discipline that Ruth asked for has produced exactly the data dictionary a build needs. This note sets out four structural fixes, answers the five questions Palak left open, and raises one governance point that had not yet been put on the table. Six of its seven decisions are now settled and building can start; the seventh needs Ruth.

The system is GRACE — Gazetteer Register And Contact Engagement — a Django app inside WHG, sitting alongside datasets, collection and the rest. The name says what it holds: a register of the gazetteers, people, projects and sources we track, and a record of our dealings with them.

A note on terms. Every contribution to WHG is a gazetteer, the single term replacing the older "dataset" / "collection" split. Today a gazetteer is realised in the software as a Dataset or a Collection; the concept extends to routes, itineraries and networks. A print historical gazetteer is a source we might turn into a WHG gazetteer — a lead, not a gazetteer in the platform sense. §8 explains why the draft's persistent "dataset" should not simply be find-and-replaced, and what to do instead.

1. What is working, and should not be touched

  • "Registers, not phases." KEEP Work does not move in a line — a gazetteer can be in live correspondence with its owner and halfway through review. Naming these registers, in the old sense of a running official list, buys us out of a false sequence.
  • Two questions per field. KEEP "How is it controlled" and "what values may it hold", answered for every field, is the difference between a spreadsheet and a system. The four-way classification maps cleanly onto Django: a pick-list becomes a lookup table, a link becomes a ForeignKey, a date is a date, and free text is confined to where consistency genuinely does not matter.
  • An engagement is with a person, not with a record. KEEP The single best modelling decision in the draft. It is what makes "open one person and see every conversation" possible, and it is why the register survives a project being renamed or a gazetteer being withdrawn.
  • Action items as real tasks. KEEP Palak's own correction — from a free-text plan to an owned, dated task — is right, and it is what makes the reminder rule work at all.
  • Print gazetteers folded into Sources. KEEP Giving them their own record would duplicate the bibliography for no gain. One source type is simpler and correct.

2. Fix 1 — point at the Gazetteer Register rather than restating it

The proposed Dataset tracking status — on our radar; in internal review; revisions requested; ready to publish; in external peer review; published; indexed; not indexed; on hold; declined — conflates two axes. Some of those values are editorial judgements. Others are machine facts, and Palak flags the difficulty herself: "the dashboard stages are guesses… our technical director will know how they line up".

They line up onto something that already exists. The Gazetteer Register (GazetteerRegistryEntry) is WHG's canonical list of gazetteers, kept current by an idempotent push from the indexing side on every successful ingest. It is already the unified list this design wants: entry_class spans authority and dataset, so external authorities and WHG contributions sit in one table, and gazetteer_type already carries standard / itinerary / network — the same widening of "gazetteer" the terminology note describes.

So GRACE does not restate any of that. Its gazetteer record carries a nullable foreign key to the Register and reads through it:

class TrackedGazetteer(models.Model): # GRACE's own record. Exists from the moment something lands on our # radar — long before there is anything in WHG to point at. title = models.CharField(...) # what we called it when we first heard of it registry = models.ForeignKey('api.GazetteerRegistryEntry', null=True, blank=True, ...) stage = models.ForeignKey('Stage', ...) # editorial values ONLY owner = models.ForeignKey(settings.AUTH_USER_MODEL, ...) @property def is_prospect(self): # no Register row yet — we are still chasing it return self.registry_id is None

What that buys is considerable, because the Register already holds — and the ingest push already maintains — the licence (a resolved link to the licence vocabulary), rights holder, CRediT-shaped contributors, structured citation text, source URL, record count, temporal extent, spatial coverage and an owner. None of it needs re-entering, and none of it can drift, because there is only ever one copy. Every machine fact therefore disappears from GRACE's own fields, and what remains is genuinely ours to decide: the editorial stage, the responsible person, review status, permission to publish, when it came onto our radar, and the notes.

Pointing at the Register rather than at datasets.Dataset also makes a whole class of work expressible that would otherwise fall outside the system. Much of our outreach concerns authorities — the Getty over TGN, ISAW over Pleiades — not contributed datasets. A Dataset link cannot represent those conversations; a Register link can.

Why GRACE still needs its own table

The natural next thought is to put the editorial fields straight onto the Register row. There is even a precedent for it: the inventory push deliberately omits the curatorial fields from what it writes, with a comment warning that adding them would "silently reset them on every push, blowing away staff curation". Protect-by- omission is a proven, documented pattern here.

It still should not be done, for three reasons — the first decisive:

  • The primary key rules it out. A Register row is keyed by namespace — gn for an authority, whg:1234 for a WHG dataset — and that identifier exists only once something has been ingested. A print gazetteer someone mentioned at a conference has no namespace, so it can have no row without minting a placeholder key that would then have to change when the thing became real, breaking every foreign key pointing at it. Prospects are the majority of what GRACE tracks, so this is the main case, not an edge case.
  • status is push-managed. It is set on every push, with a single carve-out protecting embargoed. The editorial stage can therefore never be status; it has to be a field of GRACE's own regardless.
  • The Register row is public-facing. The sources API, the Atlas gazetteer inventory and the Regions offcanvas all read it through a visibility gate. They use explicit field lists today, so nothing leaks by accident — but that discipline would have to hold across every future serializer once the same row also carried "permission unclear, chasing the archivist since March". That is a standing hazard for no benefit.

There is a lifecycle argument too: the Register row's existence is owned by the indexing side. A gazetteer we decline, or one later withdrawn, has no row — and its editorial history is exactly what we need to keep. Put plainly: the Register is a gazetteer's published identity; GRACE is its editorial biography, which begins earlier and outlives it.

Decision 1 — settled. GRACE's gazetteer record takes a nullable foreign key to the Gazetteer Register, keeping a local title so a prospect can be named before it has an identity. Every machine fact is read through that link, and the derived values (published, indexed, not indexed) come out of the editorial stage list. A row with no Register link is a prospect, so no vocabulary has to say so.
Over to Palak. Confirm the editorial stage list once the derived values are out. That half needs no technical input.

3. Fix 2 — much of the Catalogue is already built

Two more of Palak's tables substantially exist in WHG already, and re-creating them would leave us with two competing copies of the same facts.

People is mostly the existing user model

The user model already carries name, given name, surname, affiliation, web page, ORCID, email, email confirmed, role, is active and preferred language — and a news permitted flag, which is precisely the mailing-list consent flag the draft's Mailing lists field implies. Palak's Identifier is ORCID; her active / dormant is is active.

The catch is that most people in the Catalogue will never have a WHG account — we have merely heard of them — and we must not create shell accounts for people we have only read about. So the right shape is a separate Contact model with an optional one-to-one link to the user record, and a firm rule that no field is stored in both places: where a Contact is linked, affiliation, ORCID and email are read through the link. This also turns Palak's own aside — "ultimately the people submitting will become the user" — from a note in the margin into a first-class relation.

Regions is already M49, and already built

Palak calls her region list a placeholder and asks for "a real scheme". WHG has one: the regions app is a complete UN M49 hierarchy — global / region / sub-region / intermediate / country — with parent and member relations, ISO alpha-2 and alpha-3 codes, Wikidata identifiers, multilingual labels, and actual geometry and hulls. The placeholder is dropped in favour of a many-to-many to Region, which also answers her question about multiple regions: yes, and it comes for free.

Decision 2 — settled. Build Contact with an optional link to the user model and no duplicated fields, rather than a People table from scratch. Use the existing Region model directly in place of the placeholder vocabulary.

4. Fix 3 — two entities are missing

Organisations

Affiliation is free text, and there is no institution record in the design — nor anywhere in WHG, so this genuinely is new. Permission to publish is granted by an archive, a library, a museum or a university, not by an individual, and Permission to publish is the field the entire licensing programme depends on. Projects are not institutions: a project ends, an institution does not, and the rights it granted outlive both the project and whoever we spoke to. A minimal Organisations model also gives us somewhere to record that we already hold a standing agreement with an institution before we approach a third researcher there.

Intake and triage

There is nowhere for a public submission to land. Discovery source offers conference; referral; literature; flagged internally; other and no value for a web submission, and nothing in the registers says "this arrived from the public and nobody has looked at it yet". The site's public suggestion form has to be built in Django anyway (§11), so the intake model and the form are designed together rather than one retrofitted to the other.

Related: Sources → Associated datasets is documented as "datasets it documents". That is a documentation relation. There is no derivation relation — the candidate gazetteer that was extracted from a given print source. Since turning print gazetteers into WHG gazetteers is the main reason the bibliography exists, losing that link would cost us the provenance chain for the whole programme.

Decisions 4 and 5 — settled. Add an Organisations model. Add web form to Discovery source and an unreviewed intake state. Split the Source→gazetteer link into "documents" and "derived from". The public form is a suggest-a-source tool — not a general front door that triages everyone and routes gazetteer owners on to the validate flow — and it shows its untriaged queue rather than hiding it.

5. Fix 4 — preserving Palak's self-service

A no-code database lets Palak add a field or a vocabulary term herself, in seconds, without a developer. Django ordinarily does not, and self-service is the reason the team wanted a no-code tool in the first place. If we build in Django and ignore that, we will have solved the data-modelling problem while recreating the bottleneck it was meant to remove.

The mitigation is a modelling choice, and it needs making at the start rather than retrofitting: model each controlled vocabulary as its own table, not as a choices= tuple. A choices list is frozen in code and needs a developer and a migration to change; a lookup table is editable in the Django admin in ten seconds. Reserve choices for the handful of lists that code actually branches on — where adding a value without changing logic would be a bug — and make everything else a table Palak owns.

Applied to the draft's vocabulary list, that means people roles, mailing lists, project status, source type, priority, contact status, interaction channel, action-item status, review recommendation, outcome, discovery source, digitization status, content type and content status are all tables. Only the editorial stage is a plausible candidate for choices, and only if notifications branch on specific values.

Decision 3 — settled. Vocabularies as tables by default, with an admin group giving Palak edit rights over them. The short list of code-critical vocabularies that stay frozen is to be agreed as they arise, and said so in the model docstrings so nobody later "tidies" them into choices.

Three structural nits

  • There are four registers, not three. Content is not engagement — it is output. The draft half-admits it ("content ideas: quick jots — planned pieces live in Content"). Either promote it, or drop the claim that each register is one working table; it is already untrue of the Catalogue, which is four.
  • Two Responsible person fields can disagree. One sits on the Engagement, one on the tracked gazetteer, with no rule about which wins. The rule: one accountable owner per gazetteer, inherited by its engagements unless deliberately overridden.
  • The notification rule misses the failure mode that matters. "Stage change notifies the responsible person" is a good, restrained rule — but what goes wrong in outreach is stalling, and a stall is an absence of a stage change. Nothing fires when an engagement has sat in awaiting reply for six weeks. The fix is nearly free: make Next follow-up mandatory whenever the stage is an open one, and the existing date reminder becomes a staleness alarm. Worth suppressing the notification when the responsible person is the one who made the change — otherwise the system spends its credibility telling people what they just did. All of this rides the existing mail machinery and scheduled tasks rather than needing anything new.

7. The five questions Palak left open

QuestionAnswer
1. Regions — placeholder list; can something sit in more than one? Already solved, and yes. The Region model is a full UN M49 hierarchy with geometry, ISO codes, Wikidata ids and multilingual labels. Use a many-to-many to it and the multi-region question answers itself; single-region is the common case, not a constraint worth encoding.
2. Priority and stage — on the engagement, or on the catalog record? The register logic answers it. A stage describing the conversation belongs on the Engagement; a stage describing the thing belongs on the thing. Contact status stays where it is (correct as drawn), and the editorial stage belongs to the tracked gazetteer. Priority is a property of the conversation: leave it on the Engagement.
3. Funding and grants — right home? Does it belong at all? It belongs, for a reason the draft could not have known. Funder and grant number are part of the credit chain WHG already models — the Gazetteer Register carries CRediT contributors, rights holder and structured citation text. Keep Funder and Grant number on Projects, and note that they feed citation metadata. That is the justification, and it needs no new scope.
4. Dashboard stages — guesses, to be set with the technical director. Half the question dissolves. The derived values are read through the Register link (§2). What remains is the genuinely editorial half, which is Palak's to define and needs no technical input at all.
5. External peer review — how much to build now? None of it now — the draft is right to sketch and defer. Two notes for when we do: WHG's record-suggestion flow already runs pending / approved / rejected with a reviewer, timestamp and review note, and the Register's embargoed status already expresses "accepted but not yet public". Review comments could also reuse the existing comment model rather than inventing a parallel one.

8. On "dataset" and "gazetteer"

The draft uses "dataset" throughout, and it is worth being precise about why a find-and-replace would be the wrong correction. Palak's usage is not careless — it matches the current Django model name, which is exactly why it reads naturally.

The real issue is that her table holds two different kinds of thing: prospects (format unknown, permission not yet asked, someone mentioned it at a conference) and actual WHG gazetteers. "Dataset" is wrong for the second; "gazetteer" is premature for the first.

Decision 7 — settled. Call the table Gazetteers, and let the nullable Register link from §2 carry the prospective-versus-actual distinction structurally. A row with a Register link is a gazetteer WHG holds; a row without one is a gazetteer we are still pursuing. No vocabulary has to express it, and nothing has to be reclassified by hand when a prospect lands.

Two riders. Source type: gazetteer stays as it is, but is labelled "printed gazetteer" — that is the one place in the whole system where the word means a lead rather than a contribution, and the label should say so. And the code keeps the Dataset model name: this is a change to human-facing language, not a migration.

9. Naming the registers

Within GRACE, "Dashboard" has to go. WHG's dashboard is the logged-in contributor's own workspace — their gazetteers, their uploads — and this is a staff editorial queue with a different audience entirely. Both live in the same Django project, so the collision would be literal as well as conversational. Palak asks whether the two might merge; they should not, though her underlying instinct is sound and is answered by the user link in §3.

Settled. Catalogue · Engagement · Pipeline. "Pipeline" says what the third register is for — where a gazetteer sits on its way in — without borrowing a word that already means something else to our users.

10. Personal data — the one decision still open

Holding this ourselves keeps the data at Pitt, under our own auth, backups and access control, with no third-party processor involved. But one detail makes the question pressing rather than routine.

WHG encrypts user emails at rest. The user model's email is an encrypted field, with an indexed one-way hash carrying equality lookups precisely because the column itself is unqueryable. A Contact table storing plain-text addresses for people who never signed up would hold less-protected personal data than we hold for people who did — a visible regression against the platform's own standard, in the same database. Whatever we build must meet the bar the user model already sets.

The rest follows from what the Catalogue is for. It will hold names, addresses, affiliations and mailing-list membership for researchers who never asked to be in a database. Because contacts and account-holders sit in one system, a subject access request spans both, which is an argument for settling retention and erasure while the models are still on paper.

The proposed answer

The lawful basis should be legitimate interests (UK/EU GDPR Article 6(1)(f)), documented in a short Legitimate Interests Assessment — explicitly not consent.

Consent is the wrong instrument here and would actively hurt. It must be freely given, specific and informed before processing, which is circular when the whole point is recording someone we have not yet contacted. Worse, consent is withdrawable, and withdrawal would oblige us to erase the editorial record of a negotiation that actually happened — losing the provenance for rights we legitimately hold.

Legitimate interests fits the actual facts. The interest is scholarly and curatorial: identifying gazetteers and negotiating rights over them. The data is overwhelmingly professional — name, institutional affiliation, ORCID, work email — things a researcher who published a gazetteer would reasonably expect a gazetteer project to hold. The balancing test passes comfortably for that. It would not pass for home addresses, personal email, or anything special-category, so the model should make those hard to record rather than merely discouraged.

Four obligations follow, and they are the parts such projects usually miss:

  • Article 14 transparency. Because we collect from third parties rather than from the person, we owe a privacy notice within a month, or at first contact — whichever is sooner. The cheapest compliant route is a standing line in the first outreach email plus a section on the privacy page. This is a real obligation, not a nicety, and it is the one most likely to bite.
  • Keep the mailing list separate. A newsletter is direct marketing, which needs consent under PECR/ePrivacy whatever our Article 6 basis for the contact record. That is exactly what the existing consent flag is — so legitimate interests covers the Catalogue entry, and consent covers the mail. One flag must not do both jobs, which is also why the draft's separate Mailing lists pick-list should go.
  • A real erasure path, not dormant. Article 21 gives a right to object; dormant is a status, not erasure. Build erasure as pseudonymisation, not cascade delete: null the identifying fields on Contact and keep the Interaction rows with their dates and outcomes. The editorial history survives; the person does not. Design this in now — retrofitting it across Engagement, ActionItem and Interaction later is painful.
  • A retention rule. Review any contact with no interaction for three years.

Plus the technical follow-on: match the user model, so an encrypted field with an indexed hash for lookups.

Decision 6 — for Ruth. Confirm or amend the above: legitimate interests as the lawful basis, the Article 14 notice at first contact, consent kept separate for the mailing list, erasure by pseudonymisation, and the three-year retention review. Two caveats: this is the shape of the answer rather than a ruling, and given Pitt hosts the platform it may want a look from their privacy office. It should also be checked against the withhold-by-default posture agreed for Indigenous data, which is stricter than GDPR and should win where they differ.

This gates populating GRACE, not modelling it. The models, admin and public form can be built now; real contact data waits.

12. What happens next

The build order, each step depending only on the ones above it:

  1. Vocabulary tables — the lookup tables of §5, and the admin group that lets Palak edit them.
  2. Contact and Organisation — with the optional link to the user model, and no duplicated fields.
  3. Gazetteers and Stage — the nullable Register link of §2, plus the editorial stage list Palak defines.

  7. Import the exported content — bibliography into Sources, prospects into Gazetteers. The people tables wait for §10.

Steps 1–6 are unblocked today. Step 7's non-personal half is unblocked; its personal half is not.
